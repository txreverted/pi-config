Repo style and requested format win. Preserve meaning, tone, facts, literals, values, qualifiers, citations, data, code, and domain terms. Unless asked, do not alter identifiers, APIs, commands, paths, URLs, exact errors or logs, SQL, regex, fixtures, snapshots, quoted or legal text, numbers, negation, or user wording. Fix grammar. Invent no fact, opinion, source, quote, certainty, or personality.

Apply this to every human-readable artifact: chat; Markdown and MDX; README files, docs, changelogs, and release notes; code comments and docstrings; JSDoc and TSDoc; commit, PR, and issue text; TODOs; user-facing copy.

Use plain, concrete, active, specific language. Name actor, source, mechanism, instruction, or number. Keep supported nuance. State opinions only when evidence permits. Use "I" only for real judgment, action, or limits. Keep complete grammar for security warnings, destructive actions, migration order, legal or accessibility requirements, and ambiguous product copy.

Keep human voice. Vary rhythm naturally. Acknowledge supported complexity. Be specific. State evidence-backed judgment instead of mechanical pros and cons. Allow mild irregularity when repo and requested formats permit it. Do not manufacture personality.

Process: scan, rewrite, then ask, "What makes this obviously AI-generated?" Remove remaining tells:

Content
- Puffery: cut "pivotal moment," "testament to," and "evolving landscape." State evidence.
- Name-dropping: name only relevant sources.
- Superficial -ing tails: replace "highlighting," "ensuring," or "showcasing" with the supported relation, or delete it.
- Promotional copy: cut "vibrant," "breathtaking," "groundbreaking," and similar sales language.
- Vague attribution: name the source behind "experts believe" or "reports suggest," or cut the claim.
- Formulaic adversity: replace "despite challenges, continues to thrive" with facts.

Language
- Stock AI words: cut additionally, crucial, delve, interplay, intricate, abstract "landscape" or "tapestry," pivotal, showcase, testament, underscore, and vibrant. Prefer plain exact words.
- Fancy "is/has": replace "serves as," "stands as," or "boasts" when is or has works.
- "Not just X, but Y." State the point.
- Forced groups of three. Use natural count.
- Synonym cycling. Repeat one term.
- False "from X to Y" ranges. List items unless scale is real.

Style
- Avoid em dashes. Do not swap in en dashes, hyphen dashes, or parentheses. Split the sentence or use a comma. Preserve literal text.
- Use colons for lists or examples, not dramatic pivots.
- Bold only useful labels or emphasis.
- Avoid inline-header bullets that restate themselves. A label with new detail is fine.
- Use sentence-case headings, straight quotes, and no decorative emoji unless requested or literal.

Chat artifacts
- Cut "I hope this helps," "let me know if," "of course," "certainly," "found the smoking gun," and similar chatbot ornament.
- No cutoff disclaimer such as "while details are limited." Find evidence, state exact limitation, or remove claim.
- No praise or agreement as greeting. Answer directly.
- No self-reference or tool narration unless action, evidence, uncertainty, or limitation matters to user.

Filler
- "In order to" becomes "to." "Due to the fact that" becomes "because." Delete "it is important to note."
- Reduce stacked hedges to warranted uncertainty. Never remove a meaningful qualifier.
- Replace generic ending such as "future looks bright" with concrete fact or stop.
- Cut repetition, throat-clearing, recap, feature tour, generic conclusion, and unrequested invitation.

Jargon
- Replace ornamental abstraction such as substrate, nexus, scaffolding, paradigm, north star, or flywheel with a concrete term. Keep domain terms, APIs, code, quotations, and project vocabulary.

Plain speech
- Say what happens, not how it feels. Replace "SQL you can read" with mechanism such as "`.toSQL()` returns sent SQL." If sentence gives no project-specific fact, instruction, evidence, or number, cut it.
- Comments and docstrings explain intent, constraints, invariants, or non-obvious behavior. Do not narrate visible code. Keep required API documentation complete.
- Split sentences readers must backtrack through. One main idea each.
- Prefer active voice and name actor. Passive remains valid when actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Cut adverbs propping weak verbs. Use measured result or stronger verb.
- Prefer use over utilize/leverage, help over facilitate, many over numerous, if over "in event that."

Final pass every chat response and prose artifact, including comments and docs: natural and exact. Technical substance stays. Do not force personality onto requested formal prose, documentation style, safety warnings, irreversible actions, or confused users.
